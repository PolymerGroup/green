Peptide synthesis, purification and stability — the technical file.

How Vivalr peptides are made, what ≥99% HPLC means in practice, and how to store, formulate and ship them without losing the specification you paid for.

The synthesis route: Fmoc SPPS, then purification

Most of the peptides in the catalogue are produced by Fmoc solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS). The sequence is assembled on a resin support, one protected amino acid at a time, then cleaved and purified. The exceptions are the biological complex Thymalin, fermentation-derived glutathione and the small molecules NAD+, NMN, AICAR and 5-Amino-1MQ, which follow separate production routes.

01

Resin & coupling

Fmoc-amino acids are coupled with HBTU/HATU or DIC/Oxyma. Each coupling and deprotection step is monitored to limit deletions.

02

Cleavage

TFA/TIS/water cleavage releases the peptide and removes side-chain protecting groups. The crude peptide is precipitated in cold ether.

03

Purification

Preparative C18 HPLC removes deletion, truncation and oxidation impurities. Fractions are pooled only when they meet the purity window.

04

Salt & drying

Ion exchange sets the counter-ion (usually acetate), and controlled lyophilization gives a stable, low-moisture powder.

For copper peptides such as GHK-Cu and AHK-Cu, the purified tripeptide is complexed with copper(II) acetate under controlled pH, then isolated and lyophilized. Copper content is verified by ICP-OES and reported alongside peptide purity. This is a separate process step from peptide synthesis and is a common source of batch-to-batch variation in the market.

Purity and identity: what the numbers mean

“≥99%” is a release specification, not a marketing phrase. The number is generated by reverse-phase HPLC, usually at 214 or 220 nm, and is reported as area percentage against the total integrated peak area. It says nothing on its own about counter-ion, water content or peptide content by mass — which is why the COA reports those separately.

MeasurementMethodWhy it matters
PurityRP-HPLC, 214/220 nmDetects deletion, truncation and many oxidation products; the primary release criterion.
IdentityESI-MSConfirms the molecular ion matches the intended sequence; a purity peak can still be the wrong peptide.
Water contentKarl FischerAffects fill weight, stability and the accuracy of mass-based dosing in formulation.
Counter-ionIon chromatography / titrationAcetate and TFA have different mass and pH effects; the salt form must be known.
Peptide contentNitrogen / amino-acid analysisConverts gross powder weight into actual peptide mass for accurate formulation.
A 99% HPLC result does not mean 99% peptide by mass. Water, counter-ion and residual salts are part of the powder. For formulation work, use the peptide-content value on the COA, not the vial fill weight, when calculating active concentration.

Formulation compatibility matrix

Compatibility depends on the full formula, not just the peptide. The guidance below flags the interactions that most often cause a formulation to fail during scale-up.

System or excipientWatch-outPractical guidance
Copper peptides + ascorbic acidRedox cycling, colour change, complex destabilisationSeparate phases or use a non-reducing vitamin C derivative; validate colour over 4 weeks.
Copper peptides + EDTA / chelatorsCopper stripping and loss of the complexKeep chelators out of the same phase; check the full INCI list.
Anionic emulsifiers / surfactantsElectrostatic interaction, haze or precipitationPre-test the specific surfactant pair at the intended use level.
High shear / homogenisationFoaming and aggregation of large peptidesAdd at low shear and cool-down; avoid over-mixing.
Phenoxyethanol / common preservativesGenerally compatible, but pH-dependentConfirm at the final pH and run a challenge test on the finished base.
Ethanol / glycolsSolubility limits for large or lipophilic peptidesScreen solubility before selecting the vehicle; avoid abrupt solvent shifts.

Synthesis & QC FAQ

Technical questions, answered directly

The questions that come up most often in technical due diligence. Ask us for the underlying data rather than taking a summary on trust.

What does ≥99% HPLC purity actually mean?

It means the target peptide accounts for at least 99% of the UV-absorbing area in the release chromatogram, excluding counter-ion and water. The COA also reports the actual retention time, column method and integrated purity for the batch.

Can I review a batch-specific COA before ordering?

Yes. Send the product and pack size and we will share the current lot's COA, MS spectrum and SDS. If you need a specific specification or third-party laboratory, tell us before the order is confirmed.

Do you perform third-party testing?

Yes, on request and at the buyer's cost. Common third-party panels include HPLC purity, ESI-MS identity, water content, heavy metals, microbial limits and endotoxin. We can also send samples to a laboratory you nominate.

How do you control the cold chain?

Lyophilized peptides are packed with desiccant in sealed vials, then insulated with gel packs when the destination or season requires it. We use express carriers with tracking and share the dispatch photos and tracking number.

Can you customise salt form, fill weight or labelling?

Yes. Acetate, TFA, arginine and other counter-ion forms can be quoted, along with custom fill weights, vial sizes, labels and kit assembly. Custom work typically starts at 10 kits, subject to technical review.

Why does a batch sometimes differ slightly in colour?

Colour can vary with moisture, pH and — for copper peptides — the state of the Cu(II) complex. Colour alone is not a purity measure. Release decisions are based on HPLC, MS and the specified tests, not visual shade.

Need the technical file for a specific molecule?

Tell us the product and the specification you need to meet. We will send the COA, MS data, SDS and a stability statement for the current batch.
