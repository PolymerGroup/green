Research overview

The extra alanine residue changes the peptide's shape and receptor interaction compared with GHK-Cu, which is why AHK-Cu is often selected for follicle and scalp formulations rather than mainstream anti-aging creams. Both peptides deliver copper, but they are not interchangeable in a formula without re-testing.

Vivalr supplies AHK-Cu as a batch-tested blue powder with identity confirmed by mass spectrometry. It is intended for laboratory formulation evaluation and is not a finished cosmetic product.

Research focus

Published work on AHK-Cu spans several complementary lines of investigation. The summaries below describe research areas, not therapeutic claims or recommended uses.

Hair-follicle research

Studied in dermal papilla and follicle cultures for effects on follicle maintenance and hair-cycle signalling.

Scalp extracellular matrix

Investigated for collagen and matrix remodelling in scalp tissue models.

Copper-dependent enzymes

Provides Cu(II) for lysyl oxidase and antioxidant enzyme systems in vitro.

Skin-repair signalling

Evaluated alongside GHK-Cu in dermal repair and wound-model research.

Synthesis & Stability Profile

This section summarises the production route, the analytical release criteria and the degradation risks that matter when handling AHK-Cu in the laboratory or in formulation.

SPPS route & purification

Batch-released

AHK-Cu is assembled by Fmoc SPPS, cleaved and purified by preparative HPLC, then complexed with copper(II) acetate and lyophilized. Each batch is tested for peptide purity, copper content and mass identity before release.

Degradation risks & mitigation

Complex dissociation at pH extremes high
Keep formulations between pH 4.5 and 6.5. Acidic or alkaline conditions reduce the available copper complex.
Oxidative discolouration medium
Copper can catalyse oxidation in the presence of reducing sugars or ascorbic acid. Use a cool-down addition and test colour stability.
Chelating agents high
EDTA and other chelators compete for copper and should be avoided in the same phase.

Formulation & handling notes

AHK-Cu is used at low percentages in scalp serums, tonics and leave-on formulas. Because the target is often the follicle, choose a vehicle that wets the scalp rather than a heavy cream, and confirm preservative compatibility with the copper complex.

Handling summary. Store at -20 °C in the dark. Allow the vial to reach room temperature before opening. Dissolve in water or pH 5–6 buffer; protect the stock solution from light and use it the same day for best results.

What is the difference between AHK-Cu and GHK-Cu?

AHK-Cu is Ala-His-Lys–Cu and GHK-Cu is Gly-His-Lys–Cu. The additional alanine changes molecular geometry and is associated with a stronger follicle-focused research profile; GHK-Cu has the larger dermal-collagen literature base.

Is AHK-Cu water-soluble?

Yes. The lyophilized copper complex dissolves readily in water at the concentrations used in cosmetic formulation. For stock solutions, use pH 5–6 water and avoid chelating buffers.
